# Sunrise Suite: the story

This is the story bible. Every note, postcard, scrapbook page and the last letter comes from here. When you write a new note, read "Voice and rules" first. (Plan: `docs/PLAN_V3.md`.)

## The heart of it
Juliette, 30, inherits the house by the sea of her grandmother **Céline** (1938–2025), whom she always called **Mamie**. Céline loved two things more than anything: **the sun coming up over the beach**, and **the market around the corner**. And riddles. Every summer she made treasure hunts for Juliette.

Before she died, Céline prepared one last treasure hunt. It takes **three days**, and each day walks through the whole house, room by room: kitchen, hall, up the stairs to the bedroom, down through the hall to the living room, out to the garden, the shed, and round to the front garden. And it runs **backwards through her life**:
- **Day 1** is her last years and the summers with Juliette (2001–2025).
- **Day 2** is Henri, Margot and the first years in the house (1960–2001).
- **Day 3** is the girl at the flower stall and the little girl by the sea (1938–1960). It ends with the time capsule and her last letter.

Within a day the rooms also go back in time, so the story never jumps forward. Every morning Juliette starts in the dark kitchen; by the time she reaches the front garden, the sun is up.

> "I wanted to walk you back through my life the way you walk back along the beach: following your own footprints, all the way to where you started. It takes three mornings. Good things take their time." (from the welcome letter)

## People
- **Juliette** (born 1996): the player. Céline's granddaughter. Came every summer as a child; later every few weekends. Lives in the city, works too much. Never says much in the game: we see the house through her eyes.
- **Céline** (1938–2025), "Mamie": born in this house. Worked at her mother's flower stall, married Henri, raised Margot, travelled, grew old here with Chloé. Warm, funny, a little bossy, a terrible loser at cards, a brilliant maker of riddles. Died peacefully in the winter of 2025, aged 87.
- **Chloé** (born 2015): Céline's white Maltese. Small, fluffy, brave only when she has her squeaky seagull. Since Céline died she slips back into the house through the dog flap to wait for her. Juliette finds her hiding under the stairs. From then on she follows Juliette everywhere and helps.
- **Henri** (1935–2001): Céline's husband. A young sailor when they met, later a harbour master. Quiet, patient, did the number puzzle in the paper every morning. Died in his sleep in the winter of 2001.
- **Margot** (born 1963): Céline and Henri's daughter, Juliette's mother. Moved to Montréal at 18 and stayed; happy there. She calls Juliette every Sunday.
- **Rose** (1912–1994): Céline's mother. Had the flower stall at the market around the corner. Grew roses by the front gate.
- **Émile** (1908–1956): Céline's father, a fisherman. Left before dawn every day.
- **Gaston**: Chloé's squeaky toy seagull. Very chewed.

## Day 1 in detail: "The summers with Juliette"
The answers are never in the notes: a note is a memory, and the thing it's about is in the room. Each lock has its reasoning in the level file (`_why`) and three hints.

### 1. Kitchen: "Mornings with Chloé" (2015–2025) — the tutorial
- **What it was:** her last years: up before the sun, the beach walk with Chloé, the market, coffee at the table, Juliette's weekend visits and the lemon tart.
- **Puzzles:** the tutorial (it may be explicit): the market list (3 lemons, 1 cheese, 7 peaches) opens the drawers; batteries + the empty flashlight; the flashlight under the sink; the key to the hall.
- **Memories:** *Welcome home* (the hunt explained: three mornings, back through her life); *Look after Chloé* (she hides when she misses someone; she can't resist Gaston; "she follows me everywhere and pokes her nose into everything"); *The lemon tart*.

### 2. Hall: "Every summer, a little taller" (2009–2015)
- **What it was:** the summers when Juliette was growing up: the pencil mark on the door frame on the first day of every summer, the first bus to the big beach, the photos on the ledge.
- **Puzzles:**
  - *The pencil marks* (a note on the console): "I'll always remember those first three measurements we took." The marks on the door frame read 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, 15 → the **console drawer: 579**.
  - *The first bus* (in the drawer): "You always came with the bus. But we forgot what time you came, because the clock stopped working. Maybe you can fix it again." In the beach bag, an old ticket: LINE 3 · BEACH · 07:15 → **the grandfather clock: 7:15**.
  - *Our summers* (inside the clock): the summers in the order they happened, no icons: the shell summer (the sandcastle), the summer the sea let her in (swimming), the shooting stars, and the summer of the ice-cream boy → **the photo ledge: shell, wave, star, heart**.
  - Gaston is behind the photos; Chloé hides in the cupboard under the stairs (you hear her, you see a tuft of fur up close); Gaston brings her out, and the stair gate key is in the cardigan pocket.
- **Postcard:** Reykjavik 2013, in the drawer (it no longer gives the bus time).

### 3. Bedroom: "Thunderstorm nights" (2005–2009)
- **What it was:** when the thunder came, Juliette padded in with her pillow. Mamie sang the only song she knew by heart, and they counted the stars Juliette had stuck on the wall.
- **Puzzles:**
  - The room is dark: the first note on the quilt can only be read with **the bedside lamp** on.
  - *The lullaby* (on the quilt, needs the light): "First the little star comes out, then the little wave rocks the boat, and the little shell keeps the sea until morning." → **the jewellery box (music box): star, wave, shell**. Inside: a tiny key.
  - The tiny key opens **the nightstand cupboard**: Chloé's treats. Chloé sits by her empty bowl; **feed her** and she drops the storm drawing she was lying on. **Send her under the bed** (any time): she brings out the counting book.
  - *The counting book* (from under the bed): "You made me promise: one sheep for every star on your wall, and two kisses for every sheep. You were asleep before the kisses, every time." The stars only show **in the dark** (lamp off): 8 stars → 8 sheep → 16 kisses → **the nightstand drawer: 816**. Inside: the key to the landing.

### 4. Living room: "Rainy days" (2003–2005)
- **What it was:** rainy days were for forts made of every cushion in the house, number squares at the coffee table, and card games that Mamie always lost.
- **Puzzles:**
  - *The fort rules* (on the coffee table, in Juliette's felt pen, aged eight): it had to be night inside the fort, so no sunshine; but not without the stars; the shells only glow when the stars do; the sea is never where the shells are. → **the lamp panel: sun off, shell on, star on, wave off**. Inside: a small key.
  - The small key opens **the bookshelf cabinet**: the rainy-day puzzle book with a half-done **number square** (4×4).
  - Chloé **fetches from under the rug** the page Juliette tore out: *The grey ones are yours*: "Every rainy day ended the same: you finished the square, and whatever was in the grey squares was your pocket money, read like a book." → the shaded squares, row by row → **the credenza drawers**. Inside: the key to the garden doors.
  - *Mamie at cards* (in the credenza): a memory, no riddle.

### 5. Garden: "The red bicycle" (2002–2003)
- **What it was:** the summer of the red bicycle on the garden path, and the crab in the rock pool.
- **Puzzles:**
  - *The bicycle lesson* (on the garden table): "You fell off exactly as many times as there are daisies in my jam jar, and got back on every single time. The birds on the trellis laughed at you, all of them, and you laughed back." → count the daisies (5) then the birds (3) → **the garden table drawer: 53**. Inside: the water jug.
  - Chloé is panting: **give her water** and she drops a memory (the garden hose). **Send her to dig under the roses** (any time): the crab bucket.
  - *The crab* (in the bucket): the crab pinched Juliette just as the church bells finished ringing nine, and she cried until they rang the half hour. "Henri's sundial still remembers that morning. It only needs reminding." → **the sundial: 9:30**. Inside: the key to the shed.

### 6. Shed: "Bucket and spade" (2001–2002)
- **What it was:** Juliette's first summers: the bucket full of shells every day, the sandcastle contest ribbons, the red bicycle waiting in the shed.
- **Puzzles:**
  - The shed is dark: the note on the workbench only shows with **the light on** (the pull cord).
  - *The shell counts* (on the workbench): every evening they counted the day's shells and wrote the number on the bucket lid, "and you never beat that very first day." On the smallest bucket's lid: 23, 19, 21, 17, 20 → **the workbench drawer: 23**. Inside: Chloé's brush.
  - Chloé bumps into things, her fringe over her eyes: **brush her** and she drops the contest rules. **Send her under the workbench** (any time): the ribbons note.
  - *The ribbons* (from under the workbench): the red bicycle was Juliette's prize the summer after her first ribbon; the ribbons on the pegboard read MOST SHELLS · 2001 → **the bicycle lock: 2002**. In the basket: the side gate key.

### 7. Front garden: "You came" (summer 2001)
- **What it was:** the summer after Henri died. Five-year-old Juliette arrived at the gate with a bucket, a very serious face and a list. "You saved me that summer."
- **Puzzles:**
  - *The drawings* (on the bench): every day that summer Juliette posted a drawing to Papa Henri, and wrote the house number twice on every envelope, "because once wasn't polite enough for heaven." The number on the letterbox is 12 → **the letterbox: 1212**. Inside: Chloé's leash.
  - Chloé scratches at the gate: **her leash**, and she drops a memory (the first walk to the market). **Send her to dig under the roses** (any time): Juliette's first bucket.
  - *The bucket* (dug up): "JULIETTE · 5 ANS · ÉTÉ 2001" painted on it, and inside a note: "Everything you gave me that summer is in here." → **the garden gate: 2001**. The end of Day 1.
  - *The ice-cream* (Chloé fetches it from under the bench): a memory.

## Chloé
- White Maltese, ten years old, round dark eyes, a fringe she can't see through, a pink tongue that is always a little out.
- **Found** in the hall (room 2), asleep on Mamie's cardigan under the stairs. The note in the kitchen asks Juliette to look after her.
- **Finding her:** you don't see her. Now and then something whimpers softly under the stairs, and if you look very closely there's a tuft of white fur in the gap under the cupboard door.
- **After that** she follows Juliette into every room and sits where the sun falls on the floor. Tap her and she stands up, tail going, ready to go wherever you point. She doesn't always find something; sometimes she proudly brings back a sock.
- **She helps:** she **fetches** things from under beds and sofas and through gaps too small for Juliette; she **digs** in soft soil; she **sniffs** a scent you give her and follows it; She never makes you wait: whatever is where you send her, she finds. Looking after her (a full bowl, fresh water, her leash, a brush for her fringe) makes her happy, and a happy Chloé drops the memory she was guarding. She never says what she needs; she sits by her bowl, pants, scratches at the door or bumps into things.
- She never gets hurt, never runs away, and is never in danger.

## The postcards (the backs)
**Reykjavik, Iceland (2013)**: from Céline to Juliette, 17.
> Ma puce,
> Here the sun doesn't bother to set in summer, so it never really rises either. I waited all night for a sunrise and it just... stayed. Very rude. Next summer: our beach, the first bus, same as always. I'll save you the seat behind the driver.
> Mamie

**Santorini, Greece (1994)**: from Céline to Margot in Montréal.
> Ma chérie,
> Papa and I climbed six hundred steps in the dark to watch the sun come up over the sea. He did his number puzzle on the way up, by torchlight, and got it right. He says hello, and that Montréal is much too cold for a daughter of ours.
> Maman

**Marrakech, Morocco (1979)**: from Céline to her mother Rose.
> Maman,
> Margot bargained for a lamp all by herself (she is sixteen and thinks she is thirty). At dawn we climbed onto the roof and the sky went the colour of your saffron roses. Home on Saturday, in time for the market.
> Céline

**Lisbon, Portugal (1960)**: from Céline to her mother Rose, on honeymoon.
> Maman,
> We missed the last tram up the hill, so we walked all night and watched the sun come up gold over the rooftops. Henri says every sunrise is the world saying "let's try again". Please water my lemon tree, it's still so small.
> Your married daughter (!), Céline

**Kyoto, Japan (1958)**: from Henri, at sea, to Céline.
> To the girl at the flower stall,
> The sun rises here too, over temple roofs instead of the sea. It is very beautiful, but it is not our beach, and you are not on it. Keep one sunflower for me. I'll be back in spring and I'll buy them all.
> Henri (the sunflower sailor)


## The scrapbook
"Céline's scrapbook" is the look-back on her life. It has a page for each day, and on it a tile for each room of that day in route order (so from her last years back to her childhood). A room shows its period, the memories Juliette has read there (tap one to read it again), and the postcard if that room has one. Empty places show where a memory is still waiting. On Day 3, after the front garden, the last tile holds the last letter.

## The last letter (the climax)
Found at the end of **Day 3**, in the time capsule under the roses.

> Ma Juliette,
>
> You found my old tin. I buried it when I was ten, with a shell, a marble and a drawing of the sun, and I dug it up last spring to add this letter. Don't laugh: at eighty-seven, digging is harder than riddles.
>
> Three mornings, and you've walked all the way back to the little girl who buried it. I had a beautiful life. Not a perfect one: a beautiful one. I was born in this house, I ran down that path every morning to wave at my father's boat, I sold flowers, I married a sailor under a lemon tree, I raised a stubborn, wonderful daughter, I danced in my socks, I saw the sun come up over half the world and still liked ours best. And then, when the house was quiet, you came, with your bucket and your serious face, and it was full again.
>
> I wish you the same. Not the same life: your own. But just as full.
>
> I hope you will build it here. Fill the kitchen with people. Go to the market on Saturdays, the cheese man will know who you are. Let Chloé sleep where the sun falls on the floor. Paint the shed a new colour if you want to; Henri won't mind. And one day, make a treasure hunt for someone you love.
>
> The house is yours now, ma puce. So is the sunrise. Go and look.
>
> Mamie

## After the story: Mamie's shoebox
In the hall, Céline mentions the shoebox where she kept every treasure hunt she ever made for Juliette ("when this one is all over, have a look"). It becomes the side modes, outside the story, so the timeline stays in order:
- **Mamie's shoebox** (Endless): her old hunts, round and round the house, harder every time (generated, half-cryptic notes). Opens after Day 1.
- **Daily Sunrise**: one riddle a day, like the ones Mamie hid in the morning paper. Opens after Day 1.
- **The sunroom** (the hub): Juliette makes the house her own. Decor is bought at the Saturday market with seashells from the beach.

## Voice and rules
- **Tone:** warm, funny, gently bittersweet. Céline talks about her death at most in passing and never sadly; she is busy with life. Nothing scary, no guilt, no tragedy scenes. Henri's and Émile's deaths are each mentioned once, softly.
- **Mamie's voice:** short sentences, direct, a little bossy, lots of love. French endearments now and then (ma puce, ma chérie, ma Juliette), always understandable from context. She signs "Mamie" (or "Maman" to Margot, "Céline" to Rose).
- **A note is a memory, not an instruction.** It never names the lock, drawer or box it belongs to, and never says how to read or enter the code. Forbidden: "opens with", "read from…", "in that order", "the code is", "set it to", "the drawer", "the lock". The only exception is the Day 1 kitchen (the tutorial).
- **The link lives in the memory.** The memory is *about* the thing, and the player connects it to the object in the room (a bus → the clock; measurements → the pencil marks).
- **Cryptic doesn't mean missing.** When a note doesn't give the answer data, that data is in the room: a ticket in the beach bag, the marks on the door frame, the stars on the wall, the daisies in the jar. Every story lock has a `_why` (its intended reasoning, for reviewers and hint writers) and three hand-written `hints`: a nudge in Mamie's voice, which object it belongs to, how to read it.
- **The owner's examples are the standard:**
  - Clock. Too direct: *"The old clock stopped the summer you stopped coming every year. Set it to our bus again and see what it has kept for you."* Right: *"You always came with the bus. But we forgot what time you came, because the clock stopped working. Maybe you can fix it again."* (The time is on a ticket in the beach bag.)
  - Pencil marks. Too direct: *"The console drawer opens with the three lowest marks, read from the floor up."* Right: *"I'll always remember those first three measurements we took."*
- **Symbols:** no icons in note text and no ordered lists of symbols ("the sea one first, then the star"). A symbol may come back vaguely inside a memory ("the summer the sea finally let you in", "looking at the stars"); the order comes from the story itself (what happened first, time of day). The lock widgets keep their symbol icons.
- **Difficulty ramp:** Day 1 kitchen explicit (tutorial); Day 1 other rooms one link per lock; Day 2 two notes or reading the room; Day 3 split clues, chained puzzles, a note from the room before.
- **Every riddle is fair:** everything needed is in the room, the notebook or everyday knowledge, with exactly one sensible answer. The validator proves unique answers; the notes lint (`tests/unit/test_story_lint.gd`) rejects forbidden phrases, symbol tokens, the lock's own name and the answer written out.
- **Numbers that matter stay the same everywhere:** the bus at 7:15, the wedding on 18 June 1960, one sunflower every Saturday, Chloé's breakfast at 8, the house number 12, Juliette's first summer 2001.
- **Length:** a note fits on the close-up card (about 60 words; letters up to 180).
